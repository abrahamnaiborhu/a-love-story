# Storyboard — A Love Story

Status: DRAFT v0.3 — proposal for client review, not approved
Based on: PROJECT_SCRIPT.md, Checkpoint A (2026-10-24)
Changes: v0.2 replaced the window opening with the umbrella (Study 02B). v0.3 replaces the vase with **her as a flower** (client choice, 2026-10-24).

This describes what the visitor sees and feels in each chapter. It is written in words; apart from the opening (Study 02B), nothing here has been drawn or tested yet. Colours, typefaces and lines are first proposals.

---

## The big idea in one paragraph

He is water. She is a flower. It begins with him keeping the rain off her. Then he falls for her, literally, through a world of blooming flowers, into the heart of the most beautiful one: her. **She blooms because she drinks him.** That is why loving her feels so good, and also why he disappears: every petal that opens takes a little more of him. When her petals fall away and she leaves, what remains of him spills out and falls into nothing.

From keeping the rain off her, to being the water that keeps her alive, to nothing left of him. One drop carries the whole story.

---

## Shared language (continuity)

**Two worlds**
- **People are photography.** Cinematic, real, vague but strong: silhouettes, backs, blur, rain. Faces are never clear.
- **Love is illustration.** Flowers, petals and blooms are painted/drawn, glowing over the photographs. Love is the drawn, dreamlike layer over real life. When love dies, the illustration withers and only cold photography is left.

**Colour**
| Colour | Meaning | Where it peaks |
|---|---|---|
| Cobalt blue | Him: who he is | Opening and chapter 1, then it drains |
| Vermilion red | Her: her coat, her petals | Throughout; takes over in chapter 4 |
| Purple (blue + red) | Their love, "Purple Rain" | Chapters 2–3 |
| Grey | What's left of him | Chapters 5–6 |
| Black | Nothing | Chapter 6 |

**His voice (the words)**
- Real text in a **handwriting-like typeface, in his blue**. It condenses onto the scene like breath on glass as you scroll.
- In chapter 3 his letters start **reshaping into her typeface** (an elegant italic serif) and drift to red.
- In chapter 4 she finishes his sentences.
- In chapter 6 he has no line. "Mr. Nobody" appears in a **cold, neutral label typeface** that belongs to nobody.

**The drop rules**
- One drop is him, the whole way through. It is always somewhere on screen, until it isn't.
- Its colour is his state: blue → purple → red → grey → gone.
- The camera follows it. Scenes change by **continuing his fall**: one unbroken downward motion where the old scene rises away and the next one opens below, or by a deliberate cut. No zooming into the drop and no reflections inside it (client, 2026-10-24, after Study 03).

**Pacing**
- One continuous film, driven by scrolling. Each chapter ends in a **rest**: a held, nearly still frame where the line lands.
- The Play button scrolls it for you; touching or scrolling pauses it.

## Opening — the umbrella (plays by itself, about 6 seconds)
*Chosen by the client after Study 02 (02B), 2026-10-24.*

- **What happens:** night rain on a city street. She stands alone in red, no umbrella, rain hitting her shoulders. He walks in and opens his umbrella over her, tilted toward her, so **she is sheltered and his own side stays in the rain**. The title settles in the sky: *Abraham Naiborhu presents / A Love Story*, then *Scroll, or press Play*.
- **Never blocking:** any scroll, touch, key or Play skips straight to the end of the opening. Returning visitors who land mid-page skip it automatically; reduced-motion visitors see its final frame.
- Nothing has to load before this is readable; the photographs fade in when ready.

## Chapter 1 — First sight
*Feeling: tenderness, then a jolt. He meets her by sheltering her.*

- **Scene:** the two of them under his umbrella. People in photography (vague but strong); the umbrella and rain in SVG.
- **The drop:** as you scroll, rain gathers on the canopy and runs down to the edge **on her side**, merging into one drop that swells and turns blue: him.
- **Words:** in his hand, in the sky above them:
  *"It was raining."*
  *"She had no umbrella."*
  *"I had one."*
- **Rest:** the camera pushes in gently and settles; the drop hangs at the edge of the umbrella, heavy and blue.
- **Transition:** the drop **lets go** and simply falls. The camera follows it down; the street and the couple rise away and fade, and the first flowers open below. One continuous motion into chapter 2.

## Chapter 2 — Falling (the grand, flowery one)
*Feeling: rush, joy, overwhelm. The most beautiful part of the film.*

- **Scene:** open air. The camera falls with the drop through **layers of illustrated blooms** opening around it: huge soft petals blurring past in the foreground, flowers unfurling in the middle, more far below. Each layer moves at a different speed, so the 2D layers feel deep, like 3D.
- **What blooms:** wherever he passes, flowers open. First in her red, then, as he gets closer, in **purple**.
- **Her:** far below, one flower is bigger and brighter than all the rest, glowing red. That is her. Her photographed silhouette can show faintly inside its light.
- **The landing:** he lands in **the heart of her flower**, like dew. On impact the whole field bursts into full bloom at once, and the sky starts raining **purple petals**. That is the Purple Rain moment, and the biggest frame of the whole site.
- **Words:** growing bigger and surer:
  *"I fell."*
  *"I kept falling."*
  *"I didn't want to land anywhere but you."*
  …and at the landing, his first new word: *"We."*
- **Rest:** held in the purple rain, inside her bloom. **This is the belonging, and it must feel genuinely good.**

## Chapter 3 — Becoming her
*Feeling: home, warmth… and the first hint of walls (Hotel California).*

- **Scene:** we're **inside her flower**, in the pool of water at its heart. Light glows through her red petals all around, soft and alive, like being inside a lantern. Warm purple.
- **The drop:** he has dissolved. His blue drifts through the water like ink (the bloom from Study 01), and **she drinks it**: thin trails of blue rising up the veins of her petals, and the petals glow brighter as they do.
- **Words:** lines start in his handwriting and **end in her typeface**:
  *"We like the same songs now."*
  *"We wake at the same time."*
  *"I think I like that."* ("I" in her letters)
- **The hint:** the petals that felt like a sky start to feel like walls. They lean in slightly; the opening above narrows. The same view that felt like home is now closed.
- **Rest:** purple water, perfectly still, petals all around.

## Chapter 4 — Losing himself
*Feeling: quiet grief. Sign of the Times.*

- **Scene:** same flower, time has passed. The water is now fully **her red**, and it's **lower**: she drank him. She is at her most vivid, and **he is the only thing fading**.
- **The petals:** curl inward, holding tight. Their edges begin to dry.
- **Photo:** her silhouette, clear and strong. He is only a faint reflection on the water's surface, and harder to find each time.
- **Words:** entirely in her typeface now. He starts a line, she finishes it:
  *"I used to…"* → *"…you don't do that anymore."*
- **Rest:** one dry petal falls, slowly. Nothing else moves.

## Chapter 5 — Losing her
*Feeling: the break. The second big moment, sharp instead of beautiful.*

- **Scene:** her petals **tear loose**, one, then another, then all at once: a storm of red petals ripping away in the wind. Torn, not drifting, so it hurts like the glass did.
- **The moment from Study 01:** for one beat the water **still holds the shape of her flower's cup**, with nothing holding it. Then it loses it.
- **She leaves:** the red petals and her silhouette go with the wind, out of frame. The **red drains out of the water** and goes with her. What's left is grey.
- **Words:** none of his. Only, fading, in her typeface: *"Take care of yourself."*
- **Rest:** the longest pause in the film, a bare stem, grey water and empty space. This is where the visitor should feel it most.

## Chapter 6 — Mr. Nobody
*Feeling: nothing, and that's the point.*

- **Scene:** the shapeless grey water slips off the bare stem and **falls again**, down into black. The camera follows. No flowers, no photographs, no words.
- **Landing:** one small ripple in the dark. It spreads, fades. Stillness.
- **Words:** he has none. A label appears, cold and neutral, as if someone else filled it in: *Mr. Nobody*.
- **Rest:** held in silence.

## Credits
- After the stillness, a cut to black and the credits roll, slowly, like a film:
  *Written & directed by Abraham Naiborhu*
  *Have a story to tell? → abrahamnaiborhu.com*
- A small *Watch again* returns to the opening.

## How it adapts

- **Phone (designed first):** falling is vertical and phones are tall, so the drop has a long screen to fall through. Compositions are framed for portrait; lines sit where a thumb isn't covering them.
- **Laptop and short screens:** wider compositions with more blooms and more city in the opening; nothing important sits in the very top or bottom edge.
- **Large screens:** extra depth layers and side blooms; the centre composition stays the same.
- **Reduced motion:** each chapter becomes a sequence of composed still frames that crossfade; every line is still readable and the story stays complete.
